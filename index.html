Quarry Bend · Studio brief

Wheel-thrown stoneware for the kitchen shelf, made by the river in Quarry Bend.

Before June Avery threw her first pot in a rented garage, she surveyed land for a living. She still keeps her old field notebook beside the wheel.

Stoneware mugs, bowls and planters. Online & at Saturday market. Six wheels on Thursday nights.

How June sounds

Observant

Sounds like
“Ferry Blue pools darker where the handle meets the cup.”
Not like
“You'll love this stunning color.”

Plainspoken

Sounds like
“I pushed too hard and the rim folded. Here's the second try.”
Not like
“Every piece is a flawless expression of craft.”

Neighborly

Sounds like
“Come as you are. I'll have a towel by your wheel.”
Not like
“Secure your spot at our exclusive experience.”

Specific to this place

Sounds like
“Rust Bank is the color the river leaves on the ferry steps.”
Not like
“Inspired by the beauty of nature.”
Words we use
rim, river light, slip, shelf, first try
Words we avoid
exclusive, perfect, must-have, luxury
Emoji
Use at most one when it adds meaning. Keep hooks, dates and directions in words.
Caption formula
Open on one detail or question. Give it a short scene and a reason to care. End with one next step.

A map taught me to throw pots.

Before I had a wheel, I carried a field book through wet grass. In my rented garage I used the same book to write down how far a rim dropped when I pulled too hard.

Now the book sits beside the splash pan. It smells faintly of clay, and half the pencil marks have a thumbprint through them.

Watch the wobble go quiet.

Wet clay keeps tapping your palm until it's centered. I can usually hear the wheel settle at about the same moment I feel that tap stop.

The thing I tell beginners is to brace their elbows before they add pressure. That tiny change makes the wheel feel a lot less bossy.

Save this for the next time your clay won't sit still.

  • Check that bio links and Story stickers still land on the intended pages before the next scheduled post.

  • Read replies for recurring questions. Turn one into next week's Story and note why it made the list.

Three tests to run

Reel opening line

Hypothesis
A sensory instruction keeps people watching longer than a direct question.
How to run
Cut two versions of Find the middle with the same footage, runtime, cover and caption. Change only the opening line: ‘Watch the wobble go quiet’ vs ‘Can you spot the wobble?’ Run them in matched posting slots away from launch week.
What to compare
Compare the share of viewers still watching at the same early point, with the same time since publication.
Decision rule
Test thresholds: at least 200 plays per version and a 5 percentage point lead. If either threshold is missed, keep the original and repeat. Treat the comparison as directional.

Carousel cover wording

Hypothesis
A beginner question earns more saves per reach than a class description.
How to run
Use the same first-class deck with a question cover and a statement cover in matched slots. Hold the layout, caption and booking direction steady.
What to compare
Compare saves per reach at the same post age. Use booking taps as context.
Decision rule
Test thresholds: at least 200 reached per version and a 20% relative lift in saves per reach. If either threshold is missed or the baseline is zero, repeat.

Lunch vs evening Stories

Hypothesis
Evening is an easier time for locals to tap a class booking link.
How to run
Show the same booking Story at lunch and in the early evening on comparable days. Alternate which time goes first across matched weeks, outside the drop.
What to compare
Compare sticker taps per frame reach for each time after the Story expires.
Decision rule
Test thresholds: at least 100 reached per time slot in total and a 15% relative lift. If either threshold is missed or the baseline is zero, keep both slots in rotation and repeat.
